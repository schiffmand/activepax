# Tealight: the final brand

> **Status: definitive (v1.0).** This is the brand the prototype and app are built against. It is direction B ([`brand-B.md`](./brand-B.md)) as chosen by the [council](./brand-council.md), with the 13 must-change items applied and Keel's precision layer ([`brand-A.md`](./brand-A.md)) grafted underneath. Where this file and the earlier drafts disagree, this file wins. The tokens live in [`tokens.json`](./tokens.json), the mark in [`logo.svg`](./logo.svg), and the screen-by-screen UX in [`product-spec.md`](./product-spec.md).

## 1. Name

**Tealight.** Store name: **Tealight: Focus Layer**.

A tealight is the smallest, humblest candle. You put it *under* something (a teapot warmer, a dish), and it quietly keeps what's above it warm. Your podcast, playlist or lecture is the pot. Tealight sits underneath.

- **Why it works:** it sounds human, not clinical ("neuro-", "brain-") and not mystical ("frequency healing"). It holds the product's gestures (start, keep going, go out gently, start again) and its emotional promise: company, not power.
- **Trademark and search (must-change 13).** A quick web search (Oct 2026, "Tealight app iOS focus sleep sound trademark") found no app or mark called Tealight in focus, sleep or sound. The nearby apps are Tide, Pzizz, Quietude and Maloe. *Tealight* is a common noun, so before launch legal must run a USPTO / EUIPO / WIPO clearance in **Class 9** (software), **Class 41** (wellness and education content) and **Class 44** (health-adjacent services), plus an App Store and Play Store exact-name search. The store name "Tealight: Focus Layer" helps both with disambiguation and with search intent. **Fallbacks, in order:** Keepwarm, Lowlight.

## 3. Positioning

For people whose attention or nervous system needs a little help to settle (people with ADHD, autistic and sensory-sensitive people, programmers and deep-focus workers, students, sleep and longevity optimizers, and meditators), **Tealight** is a soft layer of binaural tones and gentle noise that plays **underneath the audio you already listen to**. Focus-music services make you give up your podcast or playlist. Tone generators look like lab equipment or sell miracles. Tealight never interrupts your audio, never scolds you for a missed day, shows you exactly what it's playing, and tells you honestly what the science does and doesn't say.

**The category sells power ("unlock", "boost", "hack"). Tealight sells company.** The precision is real and always one tap away, but it sits behind the warm face, not in front of it.

## 4. Brand promise

**We'll keep you company, and we'll never make you feel bad for coming back.**

1. **Your audio comes first.** We never pause, duck or fight it. If mixing fails, we say so straight away and show how to fix it.
2. **No guilt, ever.** No streaks, no "you missed a day", no comeback notifications. Totals only go up.
3. **Gentle on your senses.** Everything fades. Nothing pops, flashes or surprises you. Saved sounds never change without your say-so.
4. **The truth.** About the science (it's mixed, with links), and about the platform (we can't see what you're listening to, and we won't pretend we can).
5. **Private by default.** No account. Your data stays on your phone.

## 5. Voice

**Personality:** a kind friend who's good with their hands. Warm, calm, a little wry, never in a hurry. Puts a cup of tea next to you and goes back to their book.

| We are | We are not |
|---|---|
| Warm, plainspoken | Cutesy, gushing, exclamation marks |
| Gently wry | Sarcastic, meme-y |
| Literal and clear | Metaphor stacked on metaphor |
| Honest about uncertainty | Clinical, *or* mystical |
| On your side | A coach, cheerleader or scoreboard |

### Voice rules (non-negotiable)

1. **Describe the design, never the outcome.** "Made with ADHD and autistic people in mind." Never "helps your ADHD", "boosts focus", "improves sleep". Product copy never names a diagnosis as something we act on.
2. **Literal-label rule.** Every metaphorical label has a literal counterpart. The literal one is always the accessibility label. With **Plain labels** on (Settings), it is shown on screen, either alongside or instead. The pairs are in §6.
3. **Metaphor budget.** At most **one** candle reference per screen. **None** on Settings, What we know (science), Plus/paywall, headphone warnings or error states. Those screens are plain tools.
4. **Fire-safety guardrail.** Never imply leaving a real candle burning, or sleeping next to one. No real-candle photography or video in the product or marketing. Illustrations only. The Sleep visual is "a low ember" on screen, never "leave it burning".
5. **Platform honesty.** We never show the name of another app, track or episode. We say "Your audio is playing above" or "Nothing else playing", and on first sight: *"Your phone doesn't tell apps what you're listening to, and we won't pretend it does."*
6. **No red, ever.** Warnings use saffron, an icon and plain words.
7. **Never the word "flicker"**, anywhere, including internal names that could leak into UI.
8. Second person, present tense, short sentences, full stops. Sentence case. No emoji in product copy.
9. Name modes by **what you're doing** (Focus, Calm, Unwind, Sleep, Steady), not by band. Hz values are always one tap away and written precisely ("16 Hz", not "beta waves").
10. **Banned words:** unlock, boost, hack, supercharge, optimise your brain, neuro-anything (in marketing), cure, treat, heal, clinically proven, scientists say, DNA, chakra, solfeggio, digital drug, IQ, "brainwave entrainment" as a promise, streak, goal (as in "hit your goal"), failed, missed.

### What we know (science sheet, final copy)

> **What we know, and what we don't.**
>
> Play one tone in your left ear and a slightly different one in your right, and your brain hears a slow third "beat". That part is well established.
>
> Whether that beat changes your brainwaves, focus or mood is genuinely unsettled. Some studies find small benefits for attention, anxiety and sleep. Others find nothing, and one large study found beats made some tasks slightly harder. The most consistent evidence is for soft background noise, which shows a small benefit for people who find it hard to stay on task, and not much for everyone else.
>
> So Tealight makes it easy to find out what works for you: beats, noise, both, or neither. Your results stay on your phone.
>
> Tealight is not a medical device and doesn't diagnose or treat any condition. If you have epilepsy or a seizure disorder, talk to a doctor first. Don't use Sleep while driving or operating machinery. Keep the volume low.
>

## 6. The signature interaction: light it, set the depth, let it go out

The whole app is **one object**: a small split flame in a cup, sitting under a quiet line that says *Your audio is playing above*. Your audio on top, Tealight underneath.

```
┌──────────────────────────────┐
│  ▔▔ Your audio is playing above  ← honest indicator, dim
│                              │
│            Focus             │  ← intent word (Young Serif 56)
│   ‹ Calm · Unwind · Sleep ›  │  ← swipe or tap; one row, no grid
│                              │
│              ◖◗              │  ← split flame: wick-blue L | saffron R
│            ╰────╯            │  ← cup; its wax pool = time left
│        Hold to light         │  ← the one primary action
│                              │
│   Surface ·[Under]· Deep     │  ← depth (position, not %)
│   16 Hz · Drift · 50 min   ⓘ │  ← spec chip: tap → spec card
└──────────────────────────────┘
```

**The gestures**

1. **Hold to light (the ritual doorway).** Hold the cup for 1.2 s, the length of a slow breath out. The wick catches and the flame rises as the sound fades in over 8 s (15 s in Steady, adjustable to 20 s). It can't happen by accident, so it's never a jump-scare.
   - **Tap to start** is one switch away (Settings → Starting: *Hold / Tap*). Leo and Dev use it for repeat sessions.
   - A **proper accessibility action** is always present: VoiceOver / TalkBack custom action **"Start sound"** (and "Stop sound", "Soften: move to Deep"). Switch Control and keyboard (Space / Enter) start instantly. Hold is never the only way.
2. **Depth: Surface / Under / Deep.** How far under your audio Tealight sits, labelled by position, not percentage. It's three detents on a short track with fine adjustment between them. A **ceiling can be locked** (Sam), so no gesture can push it louder.
3. **Cup your hands (soften) is a one-tap Depth shortcut.** Tap a lit flame and the layer drops to **Deep** over 1.2 s for a call, a barista or a tricky paragraph ("Cupped" / [Moved to Deep]). Tap again to return to where you were. *Resolution note: the council graft said "a shortcut to Surface". But on the Depth axis, Surface is the most audible position (Keel's Break "rises to Surface"), and cupping means quieter. So cupping maps to Deep, and Break is the action that rises to Surface. The axis reads the same way everywhere: Surface is loud and near your audio, Deep is faint and far below it.*
4. **The wax pool is the timer.** 25 / 50 / 90 min, or "Until I stop". The pool quietly shrinks. Exact minutes are on tap, in the spec chip and in the accessibility value. No ticking countdown.
5. **It goes out gently.** At the end, sound and flame fade together over 30 s. One still curl of smoke, then *"It went out after 52 minutes."* To stop early, hold the flame ("Hold to let it go out"), or tap Stop in the Live Activity. Nothing is ever "cancelled" or "failed".
6. **Drift / Steady.** In **Drift**, the flame leans ±2° on a 10 s cycle and the sound wanders subtly inside its range (novelty without choosing, for Maya). In **Steady**, the flame is perfectly still and the sound is locked bit for bit (sameness as safety, for Sam). Reduce Motion forces a still flame in both modes, and the sound still drifts if you chose Drift.
7. **Two halves of one flame.** Wick-blue on the left, saffron on the right: the two ears. When the headphone check passes, the halves meet. That moment *is* the binaural explanation. In noise-only mode the flame is whole and single colour (saffron), which is honest and still warm.
8. **The spec card, one tap from every state** (including idle). Tap the spec chip (or the ⓘ, or long-press the intent word) and the card shows the **beat in big Lexend tabular figures ("16 Hz")**, with **L 192 Hz** in wick-blue and **R 208 Hz** in saffron, plus carrier, noise colour and level, depth and Drift/Steady. It's editable from there (see the product spec).

**Intent silhouettes.** People learn modes by shape: Focus is tall and narrow, Calm is rounded, Unwind leans low, Sleep is a low ember, Steady is a perfect still teardrop. The silhouette changes in 200 ms when idle. While lit it morphs over the same time as the audio glide.

**Other lights (deferred, opt-in only).** "Other lights are on right now" is cut from v1. It can come back in a later version only if it meets all of these: off by default, no account, the only data sent is an anonymous rotating session token while a light is on, a disclosure printed on the toggle itself, and real numbers only. Every mock-up and marketing image must label any number as *illustrative*.

**Rules**
- **Saffron means sound is on.** Idle, the flame is drawn in `waxDim` outline with a faint fill. The primary action label may be saffron.
- **Daylight saffron `#A86A00` (~4.6:1) is only for text 16 px or larger and semibold, or for icons.** Body text stays in `wax`.
- **Left = wick, right = flame, everywhere** (flame halves, spec card, headphone check, L/R labels).
- **Low glow**: automatic for Sleep and Unwind after local sunset, or always on by setting. **Luminance cap: nothing on screen above ~40 cd/m² at default brightness.** In practice the ember is the brightest pixel, text is `#B9998A`, there is no blue, and there are no white surfaces (sheets use `roomRaised`).
- No pure white, no pure black, **no red**. One glow per screen.

## 8. Typography

- **Young Serif 400**: display only. The intent word (56), session-end line, headings (25/31), wordmark. Never below 22 px, never on a control.
- **Lexend 300–600**: everything else. The **Hz readout** is Lexend 300 at 56 px with `tabular-nums`, and ear values are Lexend 500 at 20 px in ear colours.
- **Legible mode** (Settings → Text) has two options: **Lexend** (500, replaces Young Serif everywhere) and **Atkinson Hyperlegible Next** (replaces both families).
- Scale (1.25, base 16): 13 / 16 / 20 / 25 / 31 / 56. Body 16/26. Sentence case, no all-caps. Science sheet ≤ 60 ch. Respect Dynamic Type up to AX3; the intent word caps at 56 and wraps rather than shrinking controls.

## 9. Motion

1. **Motion follows sound.** Visual fades last exactly as long as audio fades: 8 s light (15 s Steady), 1.2 s soften, 30 s go out, 1.5 s pause.
2. **Breath-paced or slower.** The only ambient motion is Drift: 10 s cycle, ±2° lean, ±3% height. **Never visualise the beat frequency.** Nothing pulses faster than one cycle per 6 s.
3. **One moment of ceremony:** the wick catching on hold. Everything else is a ≤200 ms cross-fade on `cubic-bezier(.2,0,0,1)`. Depth detents snap in 120 ms. No springs, bounce, confetti or shake.
4. **Nothing moves on launch.** The first motion only ever comes from your own action.
5. **Reduce Motion** (OS or in-app *Still screen*) means a static flame, instant opacity changes and a still smoke image. Steady mode implies a still screen.
6. **Haptics off by default.** When on: one soft tick on start and one on stop.

## 11. Key UX principles

1. **One primary action.** Every screen has one obvious thing to do. On the main screen it's *light / let it go out*.
2. **Layer, never replace.** We never pause, duck or take audio focus. If mixing breaks, we say so loudly and plainly.
3. **First sound within 10 seconds.** Two onboarding screens at most. No account, no quiz, no paywall before sound.
4. **Intent first, Hz one tap away.** The spec card is reachable from every state.
5. **Literal when you need it.** Plain labels, literal accessibility labels, no metaphor on tool screens.
6. **Predictable sound.** Every start fades in and every stop fades out. Saved presets are versioned and never change silently, with a "what changed" diff and "Reset to default".
7. **Calm visuals.** Dark-first, one focal object, breath-paced motion that you can switch off.
8. **No guilt.** Totals, never streaks. "Hello again", never "we missed you".
9. **Headphone truth.** Check L/R. Detect speaker, mono, one-ear and Spatial Audio. Noise-only is first class.
10. **Control away from the app.** Live Activity, Lock Screen widget, Control Center, Shortcuts / App Intents, Android quick tile and media-style notification.
11. **Private and fair.** Local-first, no trackers. The free tier is the whole layer, with a reminder before any charge.
