## 1. Name

### **Tuck**

To tuck is to put something soft *under* something else: a note under a door, a napkin under a collar, a layer under a coat. That is the product. **Your podcast, playlist or lecture stays on top. Tuck goes underneath.**

- **The verb is the mechanism.** "Tuck some brown noise under this lecture." Nobody needs to be taught it.
- **Warm, not clinical, not mystical.** No "neuro", "brain", "wave", "zen" or "frequency".
- **Nothing to keep up with.** You can't fail to tuck something. There's nothing to keep burning and no streak to protect.
- **Short and sturdy.** One syllable. It fits an icon, a widget and a Live Activity, and it's plain enough for Dev to have open on a pairing call.

**Store name:** **Tuck: Sound Under Your Audio** (28 characters).
**Store subtitle:** *Binaural beats & brown noise* (28). This carries the search intent, and it is about focus and sound, not sleep.
**Named fallback:** **Lining** (store name "Lining: Sound Under Your Audio"). It keeps the same visual system, because the lining strip is already the brand's accent.

### ASO plan against "tummy tuck" noise

1. **Never compete for the bare word.** Every listing, ad and link uses "Tuck: Sound Under Your Audio" or "Tuck app". The bare word "Tuck" never stands alone as a headline.
2. **Own the phrases, not the word.** Target "sound under music", "binaural beats with Spotify", "brown noise under podcast", "focus sound while listening", "play noise with music". These are uncrowded and they describe exactly what's different about us.
3. **Keyword field (iOS, 100 chars):** binaural, brown noise, focus, study, adhd-friendly, white noise, pink noise, mix, background, layer. Legal reviews "adhd" (it's a keyword, never a claim).
4. **Apple Search Ads and Google Ads negative keywords:** tummy, abdominoplasty, surgery, cosmetic, plastic, tuck shop, business school, mba, dartmouth, tuck jump, tuck everlasting.
5. **Screenshots lead with focus contexts** (§14). Sleep appears once, as one of five.
6. **Web:** the domain and handles use "tuckapp" / "tucksound", never "tucksleep".

## 3. Positioning

For people whose attention or nervous system needs a little help to settle (people with ADHD, programmers and deep-focus workers, autistic and sensory-sensitive people, students, sleep and longevity optimizers, and meditators), **Tuck** is a soft layer of binaural tones and gentle noise that plays **underneath the audio you already chose**. Focus-music services make you give up your podcast. Tone generators look like lab equipment or sell miracles. Tuck never interrupts your audio and never counts what you missed. It shows you the exact numbers, and it tells you plainly that the science is mixed.

**The category sells power ("unlock", "boost", "hack"). Tuck sells company.** The precision is real and always one tap away. It sits behind the warm face, not in front of it.

## 4. Brand promise

**Whatever state you arrive in, there's a soft layer ready, and nothing to make up for.**

Four commitments, each checkable in the product:
1. **Your audio stays on top.** We never pause, duck or fight it. If mixing breaks, we say so plainly and show the fix.
2. **Nothing to keep up with.** No streaks, no "you missed a day", no nudges to come back. Only gentle totals.
3. **It won't change unless you change it.** Saved layers never change silently, including across updates. Every start fades in and every stop fades out.
4. **We tell you what it is and what we know.** The exact Hz values are one tap away, and so is the evidence, including the null and negative results.

## 5. Internal principles (for everyone who builds or writes for Tuck)

1. **Company, not power.** Copy is about being with someone, never about making them better.
2. **Engraved, not advertised.** Numbers are always present, small and exact, and never shouted. On the home screen and the layer, Hz and minutes are 16 px Thread in tabular figures. Only two places use a large number: the spec card and the Live Activity. Marketing never uses a number as a promise ("40 Hz for 37% more focus").
3. **The metaphor never appears in a control label.** Buttons and controls are literal: **Start sound, Stop, Save, Edit a copy, Noise only, Play left, Play right, Take 5.** The metaphor gets at most one *line* per screen ("Tucked in.", "Set it down whenever you like."). There's no metaphor at all on Settings, the science page, billing, errors or the headphone states.
4. **Motion and sound are one event.** If the screen moves, the sound is changing. If the sound is changing, the screen shows it.
5. **Describe the design, never the outcome.** "A 14 Hz beat over soft brown noise." Never "boosts focus".
6. **Feelings are allowed. Failures are not.** "Scattered" and "Too much" are fine. "Distracted", "unproductive", "behind" and "missed" are not.
7. **Platform honesty.** We never show another app's name, track or episode. We say "Your audio keeps playing above" or "Nothing else playing", because the OS doesn't tell us more and we won't pretend it does.

## 6. Voice

**Personality:** a friend who sits with you while you work. Calm, warm, a bit dry, and literal when it matters. It notices how you are without making a fuss. It is never a coach, a guru or a cheerleader, and never a parent.

### Voice rules (non-negotiable)

1. Sentence case, short sentences, full stops. No exclamation marks. No emoji in the product.
2. **Units are always written out**: "Fades out at 50 min", never "Eases off at 50". "14 Hz", never "beta waves".
3. **Plain labels** (a setting, and on in Sensory mode) replaces every warm line with its literal twin (table below). It also turns off the greeting, the arrival question and the texture.
4. **The arrival mapping never shows outcome language.** Under a chosen feeling we show *only* what will play. The internal "why" behind each mapping never appears in the UI or in marketing.
5. **No parental or bedtime framing**, anywhere: no "let us tuck you in", no "sleepy time", no "nighty night", no lullaby imagery.
6. **Banned words:** unlock, boost, hack, supercharge, optimise your brain, rewire, neuro- (in marketing), cure, treat, heal, therapy, clinically proven, scientists say, DNA, chakra, solfeggio, 528, digital drug, IQ, "brainwave entrainment" as a promise, "sync your brainwaves", streak, goal, failed, missed, ringing.

### The science page: "What we know" (final copy)

> **What we know, and what we don't.**
>
> **This part is well established.** Play 200 Hz in one ear and 214 Hz in the other, and your brain hears a slow 14 Hz beat that isn't in either ear. That's a real perceptual effect, and it's why Tuck needs stereo headphones.
>
> **This part isn't.** Whether that beat changes focus, calm or sleep is much less clear. Some reviews find small effects on anxiety and attention. Others find none, and one study found beats made a task slightly harder. Whether brainwaves "sync" to the beat hasn't been shown reliably.
>
> **The steadiest evidence is for noise.** Soft background noise gives a small benefit on lab tasks for people with ADHD or a lot of inattention, and little or none for others. That's why every Tuck layer has a noise bed, and why any layer can run as noise only.
>
> So Tuck doesn't promise anything. It gives you a calm layer, lets you run it with or without the beat, and helps you see what works for you.
>
> Tuck is not a medical device and doesn't diagnose or treat any condition. If you have epilepsy or a seizure disorder, check with your doctor first. Don't use Sleep while driving. Keep the volume moderate.
>
> [Read the studies (6)] [Try a blind comparison]

### 1. Arrive: a one-decision home (graft 1, must-changes 2 to 4)

- **First run** asks "How are you arriving?" with five chips: *Scattered, Wound up, Too much, Heavy-eyed, Just working*. Focus is preselected, so **Start sound works without answering**.
- **After the first session**, the question collapses into one line: **"Same as last time? Focus, 14 Hz"**, with the layer ready to pull. The chips are one tap away behind **"Arriving differently?"**.
- Under a chosen chip, Tuck shows **only what will play**: "That's Focus: a 14 Hz beat over soft brown noise." The first time each chip is picked, it adds: **"This is what we play when you pick Scattered. It isn't a treatment."**
- The mapping (shown literally, never with a reason):

| Chip | Plays |
|---|---|
| Scattered | Focus (14 Hz, brown noise, Drift) |
| Wound up | Calm (10 Hz, pink noise, Drift) |
| Too much | Your Too-much layer if you've saved one, otherwise Steady (10 Hz, brown noise, Hold, 15 s fade-in) |
| Heavy-eyed | Evening descent (Sleep) from 18:00, Unwind (6 Hz, pink noise) before 18:00. The line under the chip always says which. |
| Just working | Focus with Hold, at Quiet |

- **"Ask how I'm arriving"** (Settings) is on by default, **off by default in Plain labels and Sensory mode**, and **remembered once you turn it off**: switching modes later never turns it back on. When it is off, the home shows the last layer and the five intent names (Focus, Calm, Unwind, Sleep, Steady) as a single row.
- **The greeting** ("Morning." / "Afternoon." / "Evening.") is optional. It is off in Plain labels and Sensory mode, and can be turned off everywhere (Settings → Home → Greeting). When it's off, the line reads "Tuck" and never changes.
- Nothing about your feelings is stored unless you opt into the self-experiment. We store only which layer played.

### 2. Pull up: the ritual, with a button beside it (must-change 1)

- **The grab handle is the layer's top (lining) edge.** Its hit area is the full width of the layer by 48 pt, and it starts **at least 44 pt above the iOS home indicator and the Android gesture-nav zone**. Nothing starts from the screen's bottom edge.
- **Start sound is always visible when idle.** The pull is the ritual, never the gate. VoiceOver, TalkBack, Switch Control, the keyboard, widgets, Shortcuts, Control Center and the Live Activity all reach the same state.
- **Pull up (or tap Start sound):** the layer rises in **1.4 s**, and the sound fades in over **8 s** (15 s for Steady). The **lining strip brightens across the whole 8 s**, so you can see the fade (graft 6).
- **Level, while playing:** how high the layer sits is how loud it is under your audio. There are three detents, **Quiet · Medium · Full**, and they're also buttons. A lockable ceiling (Settings) stops any gesture from going louder.
- **Drift / Vary / Hold** (must-change 5): *Drift* lets the beat and the noise wander slowly within a narrow range (beat ±0.5 Hz, noise ±1 dB), so it doesn't go stale. *Vary* is Drift plus a different noise bed each session, chosen for you, for people who need novelty without a decision. *Hold* keeps it identical, every time. "Steady" is only ever the name of an intent.

### 3. Set down: stopping is neutral

- **Pull the layer down past Quiet and let go, or tap Stop.** The layer sinks in 2 s, and the sound and the lining fade out together over 20 s (60 s for Sleep).
- There's no "end session?" dialog and no rating unless you've opted in (the private log in Settings, or a blind comparison). Then it's one tap: Good · Same as usual · Not for me. The line reads "Set down after 52 min." for 8 seconds, then the home returns.

### 4. The spec card: tap the readout to flip it (graft 2)

Tap the engraved readout ("14 Hz · L 200 / R 214") and the layer flips over (360 ms; a cross-fade under Reduce Motion). The back shows:

- **14 Hz** in the large readout (Atkinson 61, tabular), the only big number in the app.
- **L 200 Hz** in Heather and **R 214 Hz** in Lichen. Carrier 207 Hz.
- Noise: brown, 25%. Level: Medium (0.28), ceiling Full.
- Drift: ±0.5 Hz over 10 min (or "Hold: no variation").
- Fades: in 8 s, out 20 s.
- **The session envelope**: a small plot of level over time (fade-in ramp, plateau with the Drift band as a soft tint, fade-out ramp). **This is the only wave allowed anywhere in the brand.**
- Actions: **[Edit a copy] [Save] [Noise only]**. Built-in layers can't be edited in place. "Edit a copy" makes yours and leaves the original true (graft 3). Saving says: **"It won't change unless you change it."**

### 5. The ear check: each side lights up (graft 4, must-change 9)

The mark's running stitch is the diagram. **When the left tone plays, the left half of the stitch lights in Heather. When the right tone plays, the right half lights in Lichen.** **Play left** and **Play right** are real buttons. Each one shows a *Heard* state ("✓ Left heard") once answered. When both sides pass, the two halves meet in the middle and the screen says **"Stereo confirmed."** That moment is the whole explanation of binaural beats, without a diagram.

### 6. Away from the app: the Live Activity (graft 9)

Mixing means the other app owns Now Playing, so Tuck has its own control.

- **Live Activity / Lock Screen:** the mark (small), intent name, **"Playing under your audio, 27 min left"**, the beat in **big tabular Hz** ("14 Hz") with "L 200 / R 214" small beneath, and **[Stop]**.
- **Minimal** (screen-share-safe): "Focus · 14 Hz · 27 min" and [Stop]. No mark, no colour.
- **Dynamic Island:** compact shows the stitch glyph and "27m". Expanded matches the Live Activity.
- **Android:** an ongoing `mediaPlayback` notification with the same content. It never claims the hardware play and pause keys from the user's other app.
- Also: a Control Center control (iOS 18+), a Lock Screen widget, a Quick Settings tile, and Shortcuts / App Intents ("Start Focus under my audio").

## 10. Motion

1. **Motion and sound are one event** (graft 6). The layer rises in **1.4 s** because a hand moved it. The **lining brightens over the full 8 s fade-in** (15 s for Steady) because that's how long the sound takes. Set down: the layer sinks in 2 s, and the lining dims over the full 20 s fade-out.
2. **One curve, no bounce:** `cubic-bezier(.22,.8,.3,1)`. No springs, overshoot, parallax, confetti or shake.
3. **Never draw the beat.** Nothing pulses at or near the beat frequency, and nothing flickers. **Nothing ambient cycles faster than once per 6 s** (this corrects a "slowest/fastest" slip in both round-2 docs).
4. **Still by default.** When nothing is changing, nothing moves. The optional **breathing layer** (one rise and fall every 10 s, for Calm, Unwind and Sleep) is off by default and never runs in Dim.
5. **Nothing moves on launch.** The first motion always comes from you.
6. **Reduce Motion / Still screen:** every movement becomes a 600 ms cross-fade. Audio fades are unchanged. The lining's brightness change stays, because it isn't movement.
7. **Haptics are off by default.** When on: one soft tick per level detent, and one when the fade-in completes.

A dark Loden tile. At the top, two faint Thread lines stand for **your audio**. Under them, **the layer** rises from the bottom edge in Fleece. Its top edge is **folded back** to show a strip of apricot lining, deepest in the middle, with a hairline fold shadow. A **running stitch** crosses the layer: **the left half in Heather, the right half in Lichen**, meeting at the centre with a gap where the beat lives. The mark is the main screen in miniature: your audio above, Tuck below, two ears in one stitch.

**Small-size and plaster test (must-change 10).** Round 2's mark (a pale rounded square with a peach band and dashes) was rendered at 29, 40 and 60 pt next to a sticking-plaster reference, and it read as a plaster or pad at 29 and 40 pt. The final mark fixes this in the three ways the council suggested:
- **It leads with the dark Felt/Loden ground**, so there's no peach or pale tile.
- **The peach is confined to the lining strip.**
- **The fold is deeper**: curved, with a shadow line. The layer **runs off three edges**, so it reads as a layer pulled up from below, not a free-floating pad.

At 29 pt it reads as dark-over-light with a warm seam and two coloured dashes. Neither the plaster's centred pad nor its perforation dots are present. The test page is reproducible: render `logo.svg` at 29, 40 and 60 pt on Linen, Loden and grey. **Re-test with five people outside the team before submission:** "What kind of app is this?" Any answer of "medical" or "first aid" sends it back.

- **Below 40 pt** (Settings, Spotlight, notifications): drop the two audio lines. Keep the tile, the layer, the fold and the stitch.
- **Clear space:** a quarter of the tile width on every side. **Minimum:** 20 px digital.
- **Wordmark:** "Tuck" in Literata 500 at opsz 48, sentence case, tracking −0.01 em, in `ink`. It sits to the right of the mark, with its x-height centred on the fold. Lockups are mark only, wordmark only, or mark + wordmark. Never stack the wordmark under the mark at icon sizes.
- **Never:** a peach or pale tile, a bed, pillow or moon, a sine wave, sparkles, a brain, or the stitch in any colours other than Heather (left) and Lichen (right).

### App icons
- **Default:** the mark, as in `logo.svg`. It is the same in light and dark mode, because the ground is always Loden.
- **iOS tinted:** the layer and stitch in a single tint on transparent.

## 12. Key UX principles

1. **One primary action.** Every screen has one obvious thing to do. On the home screen, it's **Start sound** (or pull up). While playing, it's **Stop** (or set down).
2. **Layer, never replace.** iOS: `AVAudioSession` `.playback` with `.mixWithOthers`, never `.duckOthers`. Android: never request audio focus, and run a `mediaPlayback` foreground service. If mixing fails, we say so.
3. **First sound within 10 seconds.** Two onboarding screens. No account, no quiz, no paywall before sound.
4. **One decision on the home screen.** The last layer is ready. Everything else is one tap away.
5. **Engraved, not advertised.** Exact numbers are always visible and small. The spec card is one tap from every state.
6. **Literal controls.** The metaphor never appears in a control label. Plain labels makes every line literal.
7. **It won't change unless you change it.** Built-ins stay true, "Edit a copy" is how you change one, saved layers are frozen, and every update comes with a diff.
8. **Gentle on the senses.** Everything fades. Nothing moves on launch. No red, no surprise sounds or haptics. Sensory mode is one switch.
9. **Headphone truth.** The ear check covers seven states, and noise only is a first-class mode.
10. **No guilt.** Totals, never streaks. "Hello again", never "we missed you".
11. **Control away from the app.** Live Activity, widgets, Control Center, Quick Settings tile, Shortcuts.
12. **Private and fair.** Local-first, no account, no trackers. The whole layer is free, and we remind you 3 days before any charge.

## 14. Marketing guardrails

- **Lead with focus contexts** (must-change 8): a podcast at the desk, a lecture at 1.5×, the commute into work, code review with a playlist. The launch screenshot order is Focus under a podcast → the ear check → the spec card → noise only → Evening descent. **Sleep is one of five, never the identity.**
- **No parental or bedtime framing**, in the UI or in marketing: never "let us tuck you in", no beds, pillows, moons, pyjamas or lullabies in creative. The blanket is a *layer*, not a bedtime.
- **No fake data.** Mock-ups never show another app's track or episode name, and never show a number that the product can't produce.
- **"Others tucked in right now" is cut from v1** (must-change 12). It can return only if it meets the round-1 standard: opt-in, off by default, no account, only an anonymous rotating session token sent while sound is on, the disclosure printed on the toggle itself, and real numbers only. Any number in a mock-up must be labelled *illustrative*.
- **Testimonials** describe experience ("I keep my podcast and add brown noise"), never outcomes ("my ADHD is better").

## 15. What Tuck never does

Pause, duck or fight your audio. Show another app's name. Start sound without you. Move on launch. Use red. Pulse at the beat. Count streaks. Send come-back nags. Put a paywall before sound. Change a saved layer silently. Use the metaphor on a button. Claim to treat anything.
