What we know, and what we don't

Play 196 Hz in one ear and 210 Hz in the other, and your brainstem hears a 14 Hz pulse. That part is well established.

Whether it changes focus, calm or sleep is much less clear, and one large study found beats made some tasks slightly harder. The steadiest evidence is for plain noise, so every fork can run as noise only.

Not a medical device. Read the studies (6)

Motion

Motion is sound made visible. The halo opens as the tone fades in and closes as it rings down, on the same curve, for the same time.

Volume and halo radiusDrift, shown as the engraving changing
  • Breath tempo, never beat tempo. The fastest ambient motion is one 6-second cycle. We never animate at the beat frequency.
  • Instruments don't bounce. One curve, cubic-bezier(.2, 0, 0, 1). No springs, no overshoot, no confetti.
  • Still mode. With Reduce Motion the halo becomes a static ring that changes opacity, and every transition is a 120 ms cross-fade.

Away from the app

Tine mixes with your audio, so it can't own the Now Playing controls. The fork lives on the Lock Screen instead.
