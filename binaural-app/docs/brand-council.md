# Brand council, round 2: Tine (A) vs Tuck (B)

> **Format.** The council has the same seven seats as round 1: the six personas from [`personas.md`](./personas.md) and a seasoned brand strategist. Each seat read [`brand-A.md`](./brand-A.md) (Tine) and [`brand-B.md`](./brand-B.md) (Tuck). They also looked at full-page screenshots of both style tiles at 390 px phone width ([`brand-A-board.html`](./brand-A-board.html), [`brand-B-board.html`](./brand-B-board.html)). Each seat scored each direction on its own, 0 to 10, in their own voice and against their own priorities. The strategist spoke last so the persona seats wouldn't be anchored.
>
> **Context.** Both directions are round-2 rewrites that respond to the round-1 council (kept below as an [appendix](#appendix-round-1-keel-a-vs-tealight-b)). They have converged a lot. Both now have the Depth control, a spec card, a plain icon, an honest "your audio is playing above" line, Drift/Steady, the full headphone check, a blind A/B test and no streaks. So this round is mostly about the **face**: the object, the name, the first screen and the feeling.
>
> **Verdict:** **Tuck (B) wins narrowly.** Raw totals are A 54.0, B 54.5. The strategist breaks the tie on position and on a category-adjacency problem with the tuning fork (§8, §9). Tuck goes forward with 12 must-change items. The biggest are the pull-from-the-bottom gesture, which collides with the system home gesture, the arrival question on every launch, and name diligence. Tine's best ideas get grafted in as listed in §10.

## 2. Maya: ADHD knowledge worker ("the restarter")

**On Tine (7/10).**
"This is way better than Keel. One fork, the last one I used already in hand, and that's the whole screen. That's the 'no cockpit' I asked for. Hold to strike is a good doorway. Depth only shows up once it's playing, so there's nothing to fiddle with before I start. 'Your fork is where you left it' is lovely. But it's still gunmetal and engraving, and I feel like I'm handling someone's expensive gear. 'Rang for 52 minutes. Rang down over 20 seconds.' is warmer than the round-1 log line, but it still sounds like a lab notebook. And honestly, every time the copy says 'ringing' I think of my ears ringing after a gig. I'd use it. I don't think I'd tell my group chat about it."

**On Tuck (9/10).**
"'Evening. How are you arriving?' and then *Scattered* as the first chip. That's literally me at 10:40. I don't have to know what 14 Hz means. I just admit I'm scattered and it gives me a thing, and it tells me what the thing is. 'With you for 24 min' is body doubling in four words. Pulling the blanket up is the ritual, and 'set it down' is the nicest way any app has ever let me stop. 'Hello again. Nothing's been lost.' Yes. Points off: if it asks me how I'm arriving *every single time*, it becomes a chore by week two. Default to 'Same as last time?' and let me pull."

**Would graft from Tine:** "The one-decision home. Tuck's home has a greeting, five chips, the layer, three detents, a readout and two buttons. That's a lot before I've pulled anything up."

## 3. Dev: programmer in deep work ("the tuner")

**On Tine (9/10).**
"The beat number engraved *in the gap between the tines*, with L 196 and R 210 on each tine in that ear's colour. That's the best one-glance explanation of binaural beats I've seen in any app. 'Edit a copy', so the original stays true, is exactly the reversibility model I want. Shortcuts, URL scheme, local-first, a Blind test with a sample-size note: all there. Two nits. First, a real tuning fork's two tines ring at the *same* pitch; that's the whole point of a fork. Making the right tine shorter for 'two pitches' is a nice graphic but it's physically wrong, and someone on HN will say so within the hour. Two slightly detuned forks is the true picture. Second, 'hold to strike' is backwards. You *strike* a fork with a tap. I'd turn on 'tap to start' on day one anyway."

**On Tuck (7/10).**
"The copy is honest and the spec card is one tap away. 14 Hz, L 200 / R 214, tabular figures, no monospace cosplay. 'Just working' as an arrival option made me laugh, because they clearly wrote it for me, and I'd switch the question off in Settings immediately. The real problem is the gesture. 'Pull the layer up from the bottom edge' is the iOS home indicator and the Android gesture-nav zone. Half my pulls would throw me to the home screen, and system gestures always win that fight. Put the grab handle on the layer's top edge, well clear of the system strip, and show a visible Start sound button when idle. Then this is an 8. I'd still be fine having it open on a pairing call: it's a layer with a number on it, not a candle."

**Would graft from Tine:** "Tap the readout to flip it to the spec card, plus the session envelope plot. And 'Edit a copy' as the rule for every built-in preset."

## 4. Sam: autistic, sensory-sensitive ("the regulator")

**On Tine (8/10).**
"One object, and it's the same object every time. 'Saved as "Desk". It won't change unless you change it.' is exactly the sentence I need. Labels are literal: Start, Stop, Save, Noise only. The metaphor stays on the fork and never gets into a button. Hold-to-strike means it can't start by accident in my pocket. No red, a lockable Depth ceiling, Still mode on by default when I ask the system for less motion, and haptics off by default. The light palette is grey-green, not cream, which is easier on me. But I can't get past the verbs. *Ringing. Rang. Ring down.* For a lot of autistic people, and anyone with tinnitus or hyperacusis, 'ringing' is the word for the thing we're trying to escape. A status line that says 'Ringing under your audio' would make my shoulders go up. Change the verbs and this is a 9."

**On Tuck (7/10).**
"'Too much' as an option is the most understanding thing I've seen in an app. It goes straight to the locked layer, a 10-second fade, a volume cap and no drift. The wool colours are calm, the serif is adult, and there's a Plain-labels mode. But:
- The greeting changes ('Evening.') depending on the time. That is a small surprise every launch, and I don't want any.
- Being asked how I'm *feeling* when I'm already overloaded is one more demand. I'd want it off, and I'd want the app to remember I turned it off.
- 'Eases off at 50.' 50 what? Minutes? Percent? Literal readers will get stuck on this.
- 'Let me tuck you in' is what you say to a child. The doc says it won't use that line in the UI, so keep it out of marketing too.
- The twill texture on the layer is busy at close range. It's good that it turns off under Increase Contrast, but I'd want it off in Plain-labels mode as well."

**Would graft from Tine:** "'It won't change unless you change it', word for word. And A's rule that the metaphor never appears in a control label."

## 7. Hannah: the skeptic ("prove it")

**On Tine (7.5/10).**
"The 'What we know, and what we don't' sheet is excellent. It's precise, it says the perceptual effect is real and the outcomes aren't, it cites the negative study, and it explains *why* every fork has a noise bed. That's the best science copy across both rounds. 'Treat Tine as an instrument, not a treatment' is a line I'd quote. But:
1. **The tuning fork is a sound-healing icon.** Search 'tuning fork therapy' and you get chakra forks, 'Solfeggio 528 Hz' and biofield tuning. The brand wants the piano tuner's fork, but the market already associates forks with the healers I'd be embarrassed to be seen with.
2. **'Something steady to tune to'** implies that something in me adjusts to the tone, which is the entrainment claim the science sheet says is unproven. 'Tune to' quietly says what the copy says it won't.
3. **The shorter tine is physically false.** That's a small thing, but it's a small falsehood in a brand whose promise is 'it holds its pitch. So do we.'"

**On Tuck (7/10).**
"The 'What we know, plainly' sheet is also honest. It includes the negative result, says entrainment isn't established, and offers a blind comparison from the same sheet. Noise only is first-class. The board's footer even says Tuck can't see what other apps are playing. Good. My problem is the arrival mapping. *Scattered → 14 Hz. Wound up → 10 Hz alpha. Too much → Steady.* The screen only shows the literal recipe, but the structure is symptom → frequency, which is how a prescription is shaped. The doc's 'Why' column ('stimulation that stops the hunt for more') is outcome language that must never reach the UI. Add one line: 'This is what we play when you pick Scattered. It isn't a treatment.' Then I'm comfortable."

**Would graft from Tine:** "Tine's science-sheet paragraph 'Play 196 Hz in one ear and 210 Hz in the other, and your brainstem hears a 14 Hz pulse. That part is well established.' It's the clearest separation of 'real effect' from 'unproven benefit'."

## 8. Brand strategist

**On Tine (7/10).**
"This is a serious rewrite. It answers every round-1 critique of Keel: one decision on the home screen, warmth carried by verbs and a reading serif, and a *specific* object rather than a generic hi-fi dial. The two-tines-two-ears story is a real piece of brand engineering. The mark, the headphone check, the spec card and the Live Activity all say the same true thing. Craft-wise it's the stronger board: the fork hero, the engraved readout in the gap and the plain icon are all excellent.

The strategic problems:
1. **Category adjacency.** Tuning forks are the most recognisable prop in sound healing (chakra forks, 528 Hz, 'biofield tuning'). Our hardest-won audience, the skeptics and engineers, is exactly the one that will pattern-match a fork to woo. The board looks nothing like woo, but app-store thumbnails and ads strip context. That's a positioning tax we'd pay forever.
2. **The verbs.** 'Ring' carries the warmth, and 'ringing' is the tinnitus word. For a product used in headphones for hours by sensory-sensitive people, that's an own goal, and the warmth goes with it if we change the verbs.
3. **Emotional ceiling, smaller but still there.** 'Something steady to tune to' is calm and good. It's still a promise about the object, not about the person. Maya gives it a 7.
4. **Name.** TINE SA is a large, well-known dairy brand in another class, and it's pronounced 'teen-eh'. Survivable, but it makes owning Google results harder."

**On Tuck (8/10).**
"Tuck keeps the only unclaimed position in the category, *company, not power*, and drops the candle's liabilities. The name is the mechanism ('tuck it under your podcast'), so the brand never needs to explain itself, and people can use it as a verb on day one. The arrival question is a genuinely new move in this category: competitors ask for a goal, Tuck asks for a state. That's the word-of-mouth line ('it asked me how I was arriving and I said *Scattered*').

The risks are real and mostly operational:
1. **Search and trademark.** 'Tuck' is crowded in search by 'tummy tuck', and **Tuck Sleep** was a known sleep brand in our exact space before it folded into Sleep Foundation. That's a higher confusion risk than Tine's dairy, because it's the same category. It needs clearance and an ASO plan, and 'Lining' should stay warm as a fallback.
2. **The bottom-edge pull** fights the OS. Fix it before anyone films a demo.
3. **Bedtime drift.** The name, the blanket and 'tucked in' all lean toward sleep. Lead the launch with focus contexts (podcast at the desk, lecture at 1.5x) so it isn't filed as a sleep app.
4. **First-screen density.** The arrival chips plus the layer is more than Tine's one decision. Collapse it after first use.
5. **Icon at small sizes.** A peach rounded square with a stitched band can read as a sticking plaster, which is a medical connotation we explicitly avoid. Test it at 29, 40 and 60 pt.

On the tiebreak: Tine is the better-executed board. Tuck is the better *brand*. Tuck's problems are fixes. Tine's two biggest problems, fork-as-woo and ringing-as-tinnitus, are in the core metaphor itself, and fixing them means removing what makes it Tine."

## 9. Decision

Raw totals are a near tie (A 54.0, B 54.5). The council decided on three grounds:

1. **Where each one's problems live.** Tuck's problems are in execution: gesture placement, how often the arrival question appears, literal copy, name clearance. They can all be fixed without changing the idea. Tine's two largest problems are the object's associations with sound healing and the 'ringing' verbs. Both sit inside the core metaphor, and fixing them takes away the identity.
2. **Who is served best.** Tuck takes Maya (the largest, most word-of-mouth-driven segment) to 9, keeps Priya and Leo, and loses Dev, Sam and Hannah only by one or 1.5 points, each for specific, listed reasons. Tine wins Dev clearly but loses Maya by two.
3. **Continuity of position.** Round 1 chose "company, not power" as the unclaimed position. Tuck delivers that position with a better symbol than the candle. Changing direction now would throw away the one thing every seat agreed was ownable.

**Winner: Tuck (B), narrowly.** The margin is thin, so the must-change list below is binding. If the gesture or the name clearance fails, re-run this council before the prototype changes.

## 10. Ideas to graft from Tine (A)

1. **One-decision home.** After the first session, the home screen shows the last layer, ready to pull, and one line: "Same as last time? Focus, 14 Hz." The five arrival chips sit behind a single tap ("Arriving differently?"). (Maya, Sam, Strategist)
2. **Tap the readout to flip to the spec card.** Tine's gesture, with its exact L/R carriers, beat, noise colour and level, Drift range, fade times and the **session envelope plot** (the only wave allowed). (Dev, Hannah)
3. **"Edit a copy"** for every built-in layer, so the originals stay true, plus the sentence "It won't change unless you change it." (Sam, Dev)
4. **The ear check lights each side.** When the left tone plays, the left half of Tuck's stitch lights in Heather. When the right plays, the right half lights in Lichen. "Stereo confirmed." Make the Left and Right cards real buttons ("Play left", "Play right"). (Leo, Dev)
5. **The missing headphone states from A:** *One earbud* ("Only one side is connected…") and *Bluetooth call mode* ("Your headphones are in call mode, which is mono. Tuck will switch back when the call ends."). (Leo, Dev)
6. **Motion and sound are the same event.** Tine binds halo duration to the audio fade. Tuck's layer rises in 1.4 s while the sound fades in over 8 s. Keep the fast physical response, but let the lining strip brighten across the full 8 s, so the eye sees the fade. (Sam, Dev)
7. **Tine's science paragraph structure:** first "this part is well established" (the perceptual beat), then "this part isn't" (outcomes), then "the steadiest evidence is for noise, which is why every layer has a noise bed". (Hannah, Priya)
8. **"Rule: the metaphor never appears in a control label."** Tuck already limits it to one line per screen. Adopt A's stricter version for buttons: Start sound, Stop, Save, Noise only. "Set it down" can stay as the warm line, not the button. (Sam)
9. **Live Activity spec from A's board:** mark, intent, "Playing under your audio, 27 min left", big tabular Hz, Stop. (Dev, Priya)
10. **Light-theme ear-colour limits written down,** like A's "icons, tines and large text only" notes on each swatch. (Sam)
11. **"Engraved, not advertised"** as an internal principle for where numbers go: small and exact, always present, never shouted. (Dev, Hannah)
12. **"Strike it, then let it ring down"** is a good line, but don't graft it. It is a reminder that a strong verb pair matters. Tuck's equivalent is *pull up / set down*, so use it consistently everywhere.

## 12. What every seat agreed on (keep regardless of direction)

- Both round-2 directions kept the honest "Your audio is playing above" line and dropped named now-playing content. Keep it permanently.
- The five intents (Focus, Calm, Unwind, Sleep, Steady), no streaks, gentle totals, "where you left it" on return.
- First sound within 10 seconds, two onboarding screens, no account, no paywall before sound, a reminder 3 days before any charge.
- No red. Warnings use a neutral surface, an icon and plain words.
- Motion follows sound, never at the beat frequency, Reduce Motion respected, haptics off by default.
- Plain icon option for shared screens.
- The blind A/B self-experiment, results local.
- A note for whoever builds the final brand: both docs contain a small motion-spec slip. Tine's doc says the "slowest" ambient motion is a 6-second cycle while its board says "fastest". The rule is **fastest** (nothing faster than one 6 s cycle). Tuck's breathing layer (10 s cycle) already complies.

### Round 1 · 8. Brand strategist

**On Keel (7/10).**
"Craft is superb. It's the most *coherent* system of the two: every colour encodes meaning, the waterline explains the product, the mark works at 16 px. If I were selling to engineers and quantified-self buyers only, I'd pick it today.

The strategic problems:
1. **Lane crowding.** 'Calm precision instrument' (Braun, Teenage Engineering, dial UIs) is the default 'tasteful' move in 2026. Keel would be the best-executed version of a look that several sound and timer apps already use. It wins on quality, not on position.
2. **It's built for 3 of the 6 seats.** Dev, Hannah and Sam love it. Maya, who represents the largest addressable segment (ADHD, which is the core of the category's growth and of the evidence base) gives it a 6. That's the wrong person to lose.
3. **Emotional ceiling.** Nobody tells a friend 'you have to try this instrument'. Word of mouth in this category is emotional ('brown noise saved my life').
4. **Name risk.** 'Keel over' (to collapse, or to die) is a common idiom. It's survivable, but it's a free headline for a hostile reviewer, and 'Keel' is crowded in search and trademark terms."

**On Tealight (8.5/10).**

- **Hold to light** is the ritual doorway.

### Round 1 · 9. Decision

Raw totals are a near tie (A 53, B 52.5). The council broke the tie on three grounds:

1. **Who is lost.** A loses Maya and Leo (the largest, most word-of-mouth-driven segments). B loses Dev and Hannah *partially*, and their objections are about specific, fixable execution details (fake now-playing data, hold-only start, softness on screen-share), not about the core idea.
2. **Ownability.** B's "company, not power" position is unclaimed in the category. A's "calm instrument" is the best version of a familiar look.
3. **Graftability.** Keel's best parts (literal labels, exact readout, Depth, platform honesty, no-red warnings) fit naturally behind Tealight's face as the spec card and settings. The reverse doesn't work.

**Winner: Tealight (B).**

### Round 1 · 10. Ideas to graft from Keel (A)

1. **Honest audio indicator (the waterline truth).** Replace any named now-playing with "Your audio is playing above" / "Nothing else playing", plus A's sentence that the OS doesn't tell us what you're listening to, and we won't pretend.
2. **Depth control: Surface / Under / Deep.** A single relative-level control labelled by position, not by percentage, with a lockable ceiling. "Cup your hands" (soften) becomes a one-tap shortcut to Surface.
3. **Hz readout as the hero of the spec card.** Tap the flame and the card shows a big tabular readout, "14 Hz", with "L 200 / R 214" in wick-blue and saffron. Same rigour as Keel, in Lexend tabular figures.
4. **Tap / Hold to start setting.** Hold stays the default ritual. Tap is one switch away, and there's always a proper accessibility action (VoiceOver "Start sound").
5. **Literal-label rule.** Every metaphorical label carries its literal counterpart in the accessibility label and, for Sam's setting ("Plain labels"), on screen: "Hold to light · start sound", "Time left", "Sound stopped after 52 minutes."
6. **No red, ever.** Warnings use saffron, an icon and plain words.
7. **Full headphone fallback set.** A's four states (phone speaker, Mono Audio, Spatial Audio / head tracking, one earbud). B is missing the spatial-audio state.
8. **Versioned presets with "what changed" diff and reset to default.**
9. **Night luminance cap** (≈40 cd/m² at default brightness) as a written spec for Low glow.
10. **Live Activity / Lock Screen control** with stop, current mode and time left, as specified in A's board, because mixing means we don't own Now Playing.
11. **Atkinson Hyperlegible Next** as a second Legible-mode option next to Lexend 500.
12. **Voice rule:** "Describe the design, never the outcome."
13. **"Evening descent"** as the named sleep protocol.

### Round 1 · 12. What every seat agreed on (keep regardless of direction)

- Five intents named by activity: Focus, Calm, Unwind, Sleep, Steady.
- No streaks, ever. Totals only. "Welcome back / Hello again" with nothing lost.
- First sound within 10 seconds, two onboarding screens maximum, no account, no paywall before sound.
- The "What we know" sheet with null and negative results, noise-only as a first-class mode, and a blind A/B self-experiment.
- Motion follows sound. Breath-paced at most. Never visualise the beat frequency. Respect Reduce Motion.
- Left and right ears get two consistent colours throughout the UI.
- A free tier that is the whole layer, and a reminder 3 days before any charge.
