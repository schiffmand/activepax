# Brand council: Keel (A) vs Tealight (B)

> **Format.** Seven seats: the six personas from [`personas.md`](./personas.md) and a seasoned brand strategist. Each seat read [`brand-A.md`](./brand-A.md) and [`brand-B.md`](./brand-B.md), and looked at full-page screenshots of both boards at 390 px phone width ([`brand-A-board.html`](./brand-A-board.html), [`brand-B-board.html`](./brand-B-board.html)). Seats scored each direction independently from 0 to 10, in their own voice and against their own priorities. The strategist spoke last so the persona seats wouldn't be anchored.
>
> **Verdict:** **Tealight (B) wins.** Raw totals are a near tie (A 53, B 52.5), but B wins clearly on the strategic tiebreak (see §9). It goes forward with a list of non-negotiable fixes and with Keel's precision layer grafted in as the "spec card" behind the warm face.

## 2. Maya: ADHD knowledge worker ("the restarter")

**On Keel (6/10).**
"OK, it's gorgeous. I'd screenshot the dial. But my honest gut reaction was 'oh, another cockpit'. Five detents, a Depth slider, noise bed, fade in/out, Drift or Steady, timer, all on the first screen. That's six decisions before I've started the thing I'm avoiding. 'Hold to start' as a doorway is nice, I'd use it. The copy is respectful and there are no streaks, which I love. But 'Stopped after 52 minutes. Faded out over 20 seconds.' is a log entry, not a feeling. The word 'precision' makes me feel like I'm about to be measured. And yes, I said 'keel over' out loud the first time I read it."

**On Tealight (9/10).**
"This is the one I'd actually open at 10:40. One object. 'Hold to light' is exactly the little ritual I need. It's a physical 'I'm starting now' without anything shouting at me. 'Hello again. Your light's where you left it.' after three weeks away nearly made me tear up, because every other app I own punishes me for that. 'Kept you company 3 h 40 m' is the right kind of number. The wax pool timer means I don't have to watch a countdown, which makes me anxious. Flicker mode, where the sound drifts so I don't get bored, is the novelty I need without having to choose it. It's not a 10 only because the candle could get twee fast if marketing over-uses it."

**Would graft from A:** the Depth slider (Surface / Under / Deep). "How far under my podcast" is a great mental model.

## 3. Dev: programmer in deep work ("the tuner")

**On Keel (9/10).**
"This is the first binaural concept I've seen that I'd recommend on HN without irony. The Hz readout is the hero. L 196 / R 210, colour-coded per ear, so the mechanism is on screen. Detents at 120 ms, no springs, one ease curve. That's someone who has actually shipped UI. The Live Activity note ('mixing means Keel can't own the Now Playing controls') shows they understand the platform. Versioned presets with a 'reset to default' diff is exactly the reversibility I want. Points off: the dial is a skeuomorphic flourish that's slower than a list for direct input. I'd want to type '14' and a carrier. Give me Shortcuts and URL schemes and I'm a customer."

**On Tealight (6/10).**
"Honestly, better than I expected. The spec line under the flame, 'L 200 / R 214 Hz, 14 Hz beat, brown noise', is right there, and the science sheet is the same quality as A's. But I'd pause before opening this on a screen share. A split candle flame in cocoa brown reads 'self-care app', and my coworkers would see it before they read the spec line. 'Hold to light' as the only way to start is a deal-breaker as shipped. I want tap or keyboard. And 'Playing above: Hidden Brain, episode 212' is just wrong. iOS doesn't expose another app's now-playing to you, and on Android that needs a notification-listener permission I'd never grant. If the board fakes that, what else is faked?"

**Would graft from A:** the honest "Other audio playing" indicator, the Hz-as-hero readout on the spec card, preset diffs, and the Tap/Hold setting.

## 4. Sam: autistic, sensory-sensitive ("the regulator")

**On Keel (8/10).**
"The labels mean what they say. 'Start.' 'Stopped after 52 minutes.' 'Steady.' I don't have to translate anything. The no-red rule for warnings is very good, because red makes my chest tight. A hard Depth ceiling I can lock, a 10-second fade, versioned presets that never change, and a 'Still' switch on top of Reduce Motion. That covers my whole list. It also looks grown-up, which matters. The main screen is a bit busy (rows of settings under the dial). I'd want a 'just the dial' view. The haptic tick on detents is off by default in sensory mode, which is correct."

**On Tealight (6.5/10).**
"I like how it feels. The Low glow screen (one still ember, nothing moving) is the calmest screen in either deck. 'It'll stay exactly the same, update after update' is the sentence I most want any app to say. But there are problems for me:
- The mode is literally called **Flicker**. A flickering light is one of my worst triggers, and the science sheet warns about seizures two paragraphs later. Even if the animation is a slow 10-second sway, the *word* makes me not want to touch it.
- 'Hold to light', 'cup your hands', 'it went out', 'the wax pool'. That's a lot of metaphor to decode, even with the one-metaphor-per-screen rule. I need the literal word next to it, every time: 'Start sound', 'Lower volume', 'Time left'.
- Tap shows a hint instead of starting. Fine for accidental starts, but for VoiceOver and Switch Control users, a hold-only control is an accessibility failure unless there's a proper accessibility action."

**Would graft from A:** the literal-label rule, the no-red warnings rule, and the "Still" switch named as plainly as A names it.

## 5. Priya: longevity and sleep optimizer ("the protocol builder")

**On Keel (8/10).**
"Aluminium, brass and sea-glass. This sits next to my Oura app and doesn't embarrass either of them. 'Evening descent' gliding from Unwind to Sleep under my audiobook is the exact use case. The night mode spec ('nothing above ~40 cd/m²') is the kind of detail I trust. The tone is a bit cold for 10pm, though. It feels like checking an instrument, not winding down."

**On Tealight (7.5/10).**
"The Low glow Sleep screen is the best thing in either proposal: a single ember, no blue after dark, 'Settling for 30 minutes, then silence.' That's an evening protocol I'd actually enjoy. Young Serif plus Lexend reads Aesop, which works for me. I'm docking it a little because the brand leans on 'company' and 'cosy'. That's less of a status signal for my identity as someone who runs protocols, and I'd like scheduling and Health integration to be equally prominent on the board. Also, a candle burning while you fall asleep is a fire-safety no-no in the real world. Make sure the copy never says 'leave it burning overnight'."

**Would graft from A:** the luminance cap spec for night mode, and the "Evening descent" protocol naming.

## 6. Leo: student cramming ("the grinder")

**On Keel (6/10).**
"It's clean, but it looks like something from my lab's equipment manual. I'd use it, but I wouldn't *tell* anyone about it. The headphone check catching my cheap earbuds is great, and 'Take 5' rising to Surface is nice. The free tier being the whole layer is huge."

**On Tealight (8/10).**
"'1,204 other lights are on right now.' That's study-with-me in one line. That's the thing I actually want at 1am in the library. The wax pool as a pomodoro is cute and it works. 'Five minutes off. The light will dim while you're away' is permission to rest without feeling like I failed. The split flame joining when both earbuds pass the check is lowkey sick, and I'd show my friends. My worry: does 'other lights' need an account? And hold-to-light every pomodoro gets old. Let me tap."

**Would graft from A:** Tap-to-start option, and the "Take 5 rises to Surface" behaviour (as a Depth change, not only a dim).

## 7. Hannah: the skeptic ("prove it")

**On Keel (9/10).**
"This is the brand that agrees with me and is still useful. Exact Hz, 'What we know' cites the null results *and* the Klichowski-style negative finding, noise-only is first-class, and it says 'not a medical device' without hedging. The board is honest about platform limits too ('iOS doesn't reveal app names and we won't pretend otherwise'). That single sentence earned more trust than any testimonial could. It looks like a measurement tool, so I'd have it open at work. One quibble: 'precision' can become its own overclaim if the marketing starts implying the precision *does* something. The Hz is precise. The effect isn't."

**On Tealight (7/10).**
"The science sheet is just as good. 'Genuinely unsettled' is the right phrase, and it has a 'Try a blind experiment' button right there, which is better than A's placement. But the board shows 'Playing above: Hidden Brain, episode 212' and 'Spotify, Deep Work mix'. The product can't know that on iOS. For a brand whose whole promise is honesty, a mock-up that shows impossible data is a credibility leak, and it's exactly what a journalist would catch. Same for '1,204 other lights'. Where does that number come from, and what is sent to your server to produce it? Fix those and it's an 8."

**Would graft from A:** the explicit platform-honesty sentence, and the "describe the design, never the outcome" voice rule.

## 8. Brand strategist

**On Keel (7/10).**
"Craft is superb. It's the most *coherent* system of the two: every colour encodes meaning, the waterline explains the product, the mark works at 16 px. If I were selling to engineers and quantified-self buyers only, I'd pick it today.

The strategic problems:
1. **Lane crowding.** 'Calm precision instrument' (Braun, Teenage Engineering, dial UIs) is the default 'tasteful' move in 2026. Keel would be the best-executed version of a look that several sound and timer apps already use. It wins on quality, not on position.
2. **It's built for 3 of the 6 seats.** Dev, Hannah and Sam love it. Maya, who represents the largest addressable segment (ADHD, which is the core of the category's growth and of the evidence base) gives it a 6. That's the wrong person to lose.
3. **Emotional ceiling.** Nobody tells a friend 'you have to try this instrument'. Word of mouth in this category is emotional ('brown noise saved my life').
4. **Name risk.** 'Keel over' (to collapse, or to die) is a common idiom. It's survivable, but it's a free headline for a hostile reviewer, and 'Keel' is crowded in search and trademark terms."

**On Tealight (8.5/10).**
"This one has an idea competitors can't copy without looking like a copy: **the category sells power, Tealight sells company.** That maps exactly onto the latent need in the research (body doubling, 'I just need *something* in the background', no-guilt return). Its signature moments are product truths, not decoration:
- **Hold to light** is the ritual doorway.
- **It went out** turns stopping, the moment where other apps make you feel like you failed, into something neutral.
- **The split flame joining on the headphone check** turns the product's single most annoying requirement (stereo headphones) into its most shareable moment. That's rare.

The risks are real but fixable: twee drift, a softer read for Dev and Hannah, an inaccurate board, and a 'Flicker' naming own-goal. The answer is to keep Tealight's warmth as the face and graft Keel's precision underneath as the spec card. Tealight can absorb Keel's rigour. Keel can't absorb Tealight's warmth without losing its identity."

---

## 9. Decision

Raw totals are a near tie (A 53, B 52.5). The council broke the tie on three grounds:

1. **Who is lost.** A loses Maya and Leo (the largest, most word-of-mouth-driven segments). B loses Dev and Hannah *partially*, and their objections are about specific, fixable execution details (fake now-playing data, hold-only start, softness on screen-share), not about the core idea.
2. **Ownability.** B's "company, not power" position is unclaimed in the category. A's "calm instrument" is the best version of a familiar look.
3. **Graftability.** Keel's best parts (literal labels, exact readout, Depth, platform honesty, no-red warnings) fit naturally behind Tealight's face as the spec card and settings. The reverse doesn't work.

**Winner: Tealight (B).**

## 10. Ideas to graft from Keel (A)

1. **Honest audio indicator (the waterline truth).** Replace any named now-playing with "Your audio is playing above" / "Nothing else playing", plus A's sentence that the OS doesn't tell us what you're listening to, and we won't pretend.
2. **Depth control: Surface / Under / Deep.** A single relative-level control labelled by position, not by percentage, with a lockable ceiling. "Cup your hands" (soften) becomes a one-tap shortcut to Surface.
3. **Hz readout as the hero of the spec card.** Tap the flame and the card shows a big tabular readout, "14 Hz", with "L 200 / R 214" in wick-blue and saffron. Same rigour as Keel, in Lexend tabular figures.
4. **Tap / Hold to start setting.** Hold stays the default ritual. Tap is one switch away, and there's always a proper accessibility action (VoiceOver "Start sound").
5. **Literal-label rule.** Every metaphorical label carries its literal counterpart in the accessibility label and, for Sam's setting ("Plain labels"), on screen: "Hold to light · start sound", "Time left", "Sound stopped after 52 minutes."
6. **No red, ever.** Warnings use saffron, an icon and plain words.
7. **Full headphone fallback set.** A's four states (phone speaker, Mono Audio, Spatial Audio / head tracking, one earbud). B is missing the spatial-audio state.
8. **Versioned presets with "what changed" diff and reset to default.**
9. **Night luminance cap** (≈40 cd/m² at default brightness) as a written spec for Low glow.
10. **Live Activity / Lock Screen control** with stop, current mode and time left, as specified in A's board, because mixing means we don't own Now Playing.
11. **Atkinson Hyperlegible Next** as a second Legible-mode option next to Lexend 500.
12. **Voice rule:** "Describe the design, never the outcome."
13. **"Evening descent"** as the named sleep protocol.

## 12. What every seat agreed on (keep regardless of direction)

- Five intents named by activity: Focus, Calm, Unwind, Sleep, Steady.
- No streaks, ever. Totals only. "Welcome back / Hello again" with nothing lost.
- First sound within 10 seconds, two onboarding screens maximum, no account, no paywall before sound.
- The "What we know" sheet with null and negative results, noise-only as a first-class mode, and a blind A/B self-experiment.
- Motion follows sound. Breath-paced at most. Never visualise the beat frequency. Respect Reduce Motion.
- Left and right ears get two consistent colours throughout the UI.
- A free tier that is the whole layer, and a reminder 3 days before any charge.
